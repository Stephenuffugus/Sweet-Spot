SHARDFALL

Art direction prototype — sprites as data, baked once, blitted like the rects they replace.

1 · THE PALETTE

Six terrain/creature ramps of five steps each, plus a reserved player ramp and four accents. Ramps are built by rotating hue slightly as value drops (warm highlights, cool shadows), which is what stops a ramp reading as "the same colour, dimmer".

2 · THE ONE RULE THAT MAKES THE PLAYER ICONIC

Nothing else in the game is allowed to be cream. Every terrain and creature ramp tops out at a muted mid-value. The player's ramp is the brightest thing on screen, and the shard-lamp at their belt is the only pure highlight. In a dark, busy, destructible world, the eye finds you instantly — and it costs nothing but discipline about which hex values get used where.

4 · SILHOUETTE TEST

The real test of a roster: black out every sprite and see whether you can still name them. If two silhouettes read the same, one of them is redundant and needs a different top shape, stance or proportion.

5 · IN CONTEXT

Sprites against real terrain, with the darkness and the light radius the game actually uses. This is the frame that matters — a sprite that reads on a white page and vanishes in a cave is a failed sprite.
