Do not start by adding content

§2 lists five structural faults, three of them confirmed by measurement. Every one of them makes new content worth less than it looks.

02 The five things that actually matter

Ranked by how much they devalue everything else.

2.1 · The descent has no shape Measured

Sampling a 40×40 tile block at the middle of each biome band:

caves0.0550.904

The caves band is 90% air — an open void, not caves. Then the world abruptly becomes more solid and stays flat for the remaining 2,800 tiles. No opening out, no tightening, no rhythm. The code comment claims density “grows a touch with depth”; it does the opposite, and then does nothing.

2.2 · Boss phases do nothing Confirmed

bossPhase() sets e.pat = next.pat, and e.pat is never read anywhere in the file. The pattern fires once as a burst on transition, then the boss reverts to identical behaviour. The comment above it claims e.pat “then colors ongoing behaviour”. It does not.

So the five minibosses are still, functionally, the HP sponges the original handoff complained about — now with a roar and a brief invulnerability.

2.3 · Every telegraph draws the wrong distance Measured

The whole point of the windup system is that a hit is readable before it lands, and the ground marker shows atk.range. But the enemy lunges on the active frame, so real reach is range + lunge × act:

EnemyMarker showsActual reachLie factor
rockling26933.58×
stalker24793.30×
wraith28822.94×
ember22642.91×
voidmaw401082.71×
all 19 enemies range 1.6× – 3.6×

Standing just outside the marker is not safe on any enemy in the game. This teaches the player the wrong thing, which is worse than having no marker at all.

2.4 · Loot has no depth axis

mkItem(baseId, rarity) takes no item level. Affix ranges are constant forever, the base pool is unlock-gated but not depth-gated, and rarity odds are fixed at the drop site. Only shard count scales with depth.

A sword found at 3,000 m is statistically identical to one found at 50 m — which quietly removes the entire reason an ARPG exists.

2.5 · Everything looks the same because everything is a rectangle

Flat quads with a face dot. A rockling and a crawler differ only by hue, and under the depth darkness at 1,500 m they differ by almost nothing. This is a fairness problem before it is an aesthetic one — you cannot react to a threat you cannot identify.

03 Art direction Decided

Real pixel art, no asset files, no build step.

Sprites are data, baked once

One string per pixel row, characters indexing a palette ramp, baked into offscreen canvases at load and blitted thereafter. Runtime cost equals the fillRect it replaces. Adding an enemy stays a table entry. Full roster projects to 40–60 KB of readable, diffable source, against a current file of ~150 KB.

One rig, many skins

Dead Cells' real saving wasn't 3D — it was reusing one skeleton across every monster. Its artist says that alone “spared me hundred of hours”. We can't ship a 3D pipeline, but we can ship that idea: three parametric rigs — humanoid, quadruped, floater — and each of the 19 enemies becomes a (rig, proportions, ramp, top-shape, scale) tuple.

The palette

Six five-step ramps plus one reserved player ramp and four accents, with hue rotating warm→cool as value drops — which is what stops a ramp reading as one colour at five brightnesses.

The rule that does most of the work

Nothing in the game may be cream except the player. Every terrain and creature ramp tops out at a muted mid-value. In a dark, destructible, visually busy world the eye finds you instantly — and it costs nothing but discipline about which hex values go where.

The research pass built and measured a 63-colour version against the real WCAG luminance formula. Its first attempt failed — a rust enemy at 1.14:1 against lit terrain — and the corrected version clears 3.92:1 against terrain base and 2.61:1 against the brightest terrain highlight. Use the measured values, not the prototype's first pass.

Silhouette law

Every entity family gets a mandatory unique top-shape in the top two rows of its sprite box, and a unique ground-contact count. Player: single tuft, 2 legs. Beast: two horns with a gap, 4 legs. Construct: flat overhanging top, 2 wide blocks. Swarm: domed, no top feature, 4 thin. Wraith: single tapered spike, floats with a visible gap.

The test is mechanical: black-fill every sprite and name them. If two read the same, one is redundant.

Animation budget

Keyposes first, VFX over frames. Dead Cells is pose-to-pose at 30 fps with interpolation only outside keys. A 2-keypose attack with an arc streak, hitstop and a flash reads better than an 8-frame attack with none. Budget: idle 2, walk 4, attack 3 (windup / strike / recover), hit 1, death 3.

04 Combat maths Decided

Keep the shape, cap the extremes, split the curves.

Keep flat armour, but cap it

max(1, dmg − armor) is the right shape — strong against swarms, weak against one big hit — and it's what differentiates Vanguard from Marksman. But unbounded flat subtraction against depth-scaled damage inverts at depth.

Decision: keep flat subtraction, add a floor of 15% of incoming damage. taken = max(dmg × 0.15, dmg − armor). Armour can remove at most 85% of a hit, ever.

Split HP and damage scaling Proposal

Today depthMul = 1 + depth/900 reaches 4.5× at the floor and is applied to HP and damage. Linear scaling on both axes degrades time-to-kill and time-to-die together, which flattens the curve into a wall. Split them, so deep enemies are tougher rather than deadlier:

05 The sprint plan

Ordered by dependency, not by appeal. Each ends green on all suites.

Sprint 0 Fix what's broken

Small, and it comes first because everything else is worth less until it's done.

  1. Telegraph honesty. Draw the real reach, and cut the worst offenders (rockling 420→260, stalker 460→300) so the tell is readable rather than merely accurate.
  2. Boss patterns. Make e.pat do something, or delete it and stop claiming it does.
  3. World shape. Give the descent the arc in §6.
  4. Windup floor. Human reaction is ~250 ms; several grunts sit at 220 ms and Threat III multiplies windups by 0.8, pushing 11 of 14 below it. Hard floor of 0.26 s after all multipliers; Threat III shortens recovery instead.
  5. Add a recovery state. Tell → punish window → reset is two-thirds built: there's no recovery, so there's no punish window.

Exit — all suites green, plus assertions for marker accuracy, the windup floor, and e.pat actually changing behaviour.

Sprint 1 Art foundation

Prove the pipeline on one band before committing the roster.

  1. Port the sprite baker behind the existing drawEntity() seam — player and the four caves-band enemies only.
  2. Adopt the WCAG-measured palette. Add a contrast assertion: every actor ramp against every terrain ramp must clear 3:1. A test, not a guideline.
  3. Retile terrain with the lit-edge treatment (lit top and left, shadowed base and right).
  4. Three parametric rigs, so the remaining 15 enemies are cheap.

Exit — the caves band looks finished; the rest still renders on the rect fallback.

Sprint 2 Art rollout

The remaining roster, gear, pickups, POIs and per-biome tile sets.

Exit — black-fill every sprite; all 19 nameable, no two sharing a top-shape within a band.

Sprint 3 Combat maths and difficulty

Make balance measurable.

  1. Split HP/damage scaling; armour floor; crit multiplier soft cap at 4.0×.
  2. Build the TTK harness — assemble a representative build per depth and assert hits-to-kill and hits-to-die land in band.
  3. Rebalance Threat tiers against the new curves.

Exit — the TTK harness passes at every depth, for every class.

Sprint 4 Itemisation with a depth axis

The missing ARPG spine. A real refactor.

  1. mkItem(baseId, rarity, ilvl), where ilvl derives from drop depth.
  2. Affix tiers — five per affix with ascending ranges, gated by ilvl.
  3. Depth-gate base availability; scale rarity odds with depth and Threat.
  4. Fewer, better drops: target 8–12 items per run, 2–3 worth a look, 1 worth equipping.

Exit — a 3,000 m item is visibly and statistically better than a 100 m item.

Sprint 5 Boss and enemy design

Three real phases, and roles the roster is missing.

  1. Three distinct phases per miniboss, not one burst plus a colour change.
  2. The roster over-indexes on rushers; missing are area-denial, a healer/buffer, and a positional threat.
  3. Threat-budget per spawn group rather than uniform random, so fights have shape.
  4. Audit elite modifiers for the ones that feel unfair.

Exit — every boss fight has three recognisably different phases.

Sprint 6 Skill, gear and buff depth

Supports that change a skill's contract, not just its numbers.

  1. Rework supports so each answers “what does this let the skill do that it couldn't?”
  2. Audit for soup: 62 gems × 22 attunements × 12 boons × 15 tree nodes is a lot of stacking. Explicit power budgets per source.
  3. Build an archetype validator — construct each of the 8 target builds headlessly and assert it reaches viable numbers at 1,500 m.

Exit — all 8 archetypes validate, and no build exceeds 1.6× the weakest viable one.

Sprint 7 Game feel

Every number here is currently wrong in a specific, measured way.

  1. Screenshake is 5.9% of screen width at max (16 px on a 272 px view). Hades is 0.4%. Cap at 8 px, trauma² decay, distance falloff — and stop shaking on ordinary hits. In Hades only 3 of 207 enemies shake the screen on death, all explosive.
  2. Add player-damage hitstop. Hades gives ~10–40 ms at 10% speed for dealing damage and ~140 ms at 1% for taking it. We have none. Highest-value single feel change available.
  3. Rewrite hitstop as a time-scale system — the impact frame must play at full speed before the freeze, and the freeze must never be a hard zero.
  4. Buffer and forgiveness timers must run on unscaled time. A 160 ms hitstop currently eats the entire 120 ms jump buffer.
  5. Widen the camera toward 22 tiles. At 17 the player is 13% of screen height — twice Celeste's ratio, in a game with flight and ranged enemies. Prototype first; it changes engagement ranges.
  6. Coalesce damage numbers by source; never float one for damage taken.
  7. Voice budget 6 → 16 with per-class caps, a compressor, and panning by screen position.
  8. prefers-reduced-motion, intensity sliders, and Okabe–Ito damage colours — which work because they vary luminance, not just hue, so it's a palette swap rather than a redesign.

Exit — shot-for-shot comparisons, and a feel checklist in the handoff.

Sprint 8 World structure

A descent you can feel the shape of with the HUD off.

  1. Implement the §6 arc.
  2. Room templates stamped into the noise. Spelunky ships a small template set and gets enormous variety from placement; we have none.
  3. A hazard and a traversal rule unique to each band, so a biome isn't a palette swap.
  4. Signposting so secrets are findable rather than lucky.

Exit — a blind descent has a felt rhythm.

Sprint 9 Content wave 2, then ship

Fill the gaps the archetype validator exposes, tune against the TTK harness, release.

07 Decisions for Stephen

Five forks where my recommendation shouldn't be the last word.

  1. Camera width. Research says 17 tiles is roughly half what a game with flight and ranged enemies wants. But widening changes every engagement range. Recommendation: dynamic — 17 at rest, 22 when moving fast.
  2. Art scope. The roster is ~2 sprints. Worth it now, or ship mechanics first? Recommendation: now. Everything else is invisible until the game reads.
  3. How hard should this be? 6–9 hits to die is Hades-ish; 3–4 would be Dead Cells-ish. Recommendation: 6–9, because flight and destructible terrain give you more ways to make mistakes.
  4. Does the Vault stay? It's the one thing softening the roguelite loop. Built and working — but the first thing to cut if art and feel work make the game easier.
  5. Sky Wolf Studios. Shardfall hasn't been raised with the Director. If it joins the sunbeams economy, shards need a conversion story — and that's a Sprint 4 dependency, not an afterthought.

08 What this plan is not

It is not a promise that the game will be good.

Every number here is either sourced from a shipped game or marked as a proposal, and none of it has been tested against a person playing this game. The plan's own §1 says the largest risk is that nobody has played it, and nine sprints of work do not change that.

The highest-value thing that could happen next

Somebody spends ten minutes with it on a phone and writes down what felt bad.
